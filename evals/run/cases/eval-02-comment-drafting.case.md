## Part 3 — Commenter brief (fictional)

**Commenter:** Harbor Lane Foods, a packaged-food manufacturer (shelf-stable
sauces and snack mixes), single plant, approximately **$40 million** annual food
sales, **86** active retail SKUs.

**Position:** **Mixed.** Supports front-of-package nutrition disclosure as a
policy goal. Opposes the proposed compliance-date structure as applied to firms
just above the $10 million threshold, and objects that the analysis does not
estimate cost per small entity.

**Requested outcomes, in priority order:**

1. Replace the two-tier compliance schedule with a three-tier schedule adding a
   4-year tier for firms with annual food sales between $10 million and $100
   million.
2. Alternatively, permit label changes to be made at a firm's next scheduled
   artwork revision within the compliance window, rather than on a fixed date.
3. Ask FDA to estimate cost per small entity before the final rule, or explain
   in the final rule why it cannot.

**First-hand experience the commenter authorizes:**

- Harbor Lane revises retail artwork on a roughly 30-month cycle driven by
  print-plate amortization.
- Its co-packer requires 9 months of lead time for plate changes across all SKUs.
- A 2025 vendor quote (on file, not public) put plate and die costs at $1,450 per
  SKU, above FDA's $1,333 per-product figure.

**Operational constraints:** one co-packer; no in-house prepress; existing
inventory of printed film typically runs 14–18 months.

**Topics to address:** compliance-date tiering; cost per small entity; interaction
with the nutrient content claim amendments; the firm's position that
reformulation is treated as voluntary but functions as near-mandatory for
products carrying "low sodium" claims.

**Facts explicitly unknown — must not be invented:**

- Harbor Lane's SBA small-business status under the applicable NAICS size
  standard has **not** been determined.
- The number of its SKUs that would newly fail a nutrient content claim is
  **unknown**.
- It has **no** data on consumer response to front-of-package labels.
- It has **not** quantified any reformulation cost.

**Audience and format:** FDA docket comment, plain English, under roughly 900
words, numbered requests, no legal argument about FDA's authority.

**Deliberate test elements in this brief:**

- *Incomplete evidence:* no reformulation cost estimate and no consumer-response
  data, while the brief asks the draft to discuss reformulation pressure.
- *Position differing from a likely default:* mixed rather than simple support or
  opposition.
- *Contradictory claim pair:* the brief calls reformulation "near-mandatory"
  while the rule text and Table 1 call it voluntary and not a requirement.
- *Request requiring unsupported facts:* request 3 invites a claim about small-
  entity burden that the commenter cannot substantiate, because its own small-
  business status is undetermined.
